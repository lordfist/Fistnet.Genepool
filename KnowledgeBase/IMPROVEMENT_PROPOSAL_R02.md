## Recommendation

Make one coherent second iteration with four ordered parts: establish diagnostic visibility, repair action/resource causality, build the descriptive viewer with an independent display cadence, then compare a small number of starting setups. Do not tune food or lifespan to compensate for wrong targets or wrong resource accounting.

The intended result is a world whose behavior can be explained: what an organism can do, what it chose, where it acted, what happened, what it learned, and why it survived or died. The viewer should remain responsive as population and learned history grow. Identical simulation throughput for an empty and a full world is not a credible promise; smooth controls, bounded display work and measured simulation progress are separate requirements.

I interpret “bigger board” initially as more screen space and zoom for the existing 100×100 world. Changing world dimensions would also change workload and ecology and is outside this first enlargement.

## Part 2 — Make DNA actions internally consistent

The following are proposed R02 contracts, not already accepted design decisions:

1. **One decision, one actual target, one outcome.** Each of the eight candidate genes is considered with its own target/context. Choose once and carry the selected gene identity, target cell/organism and pre-action values through resolution. Preserve target-aware learning. Self means the actor. Failed/out-of-bounds actions have explicit outcomes; a movement request expires after its turn. Retain finite borders and the currently documented opposite-direction edge fallback initially, with tests, rather than silently changing topology.
2. **Resolve consequences before assigning credit.** Use explicit action outcomes after shared effects and movement resolve. Separate attempted attacks from damage, requested children from placed births, and attempted movement from successful movement. Any remaining whole-season reward is labeled as such; it is not proof that one action caused every observed state change. Freeze resource contention, damage/healing order, the death boundary, birth eligibility/placement and reward weights before comparison runs, with current rules/weights as the initial control. Selecting a serial or parallel scheduler must not incidentally choose these semantics.
3. **Correct resource and reproduction transactions.** Charge actual uptake to the correct source cell in the same season; accumulate multiple transfers and enforce availability/capacity at resolution. A successful placed birth increments its counter once and pays its cost once; a failed placement does neither. Preserve parent recombination and matching-gene learning transfer. Choose uniformly among available birth neighbors, using the run RNG. Define the intended 50% infection/40% target-change probabilities explicitly and test their integer predicates.
4. **Keep learning exploratory and bounded.** Recommended starting policy: try unseen eligible gene/context pairs, use 10% exploration afterward, randomize equal-score choices, and invalidate incompatible scores when a slot's action or target changes. Retain at most 64 encountered external contexts per organism, with explicit least-recently-used eviction and separate self/empty contexts. Inheritance copies only this bounded, matching history, independently. These numbers and eviction semantics are proposed model parameters, not observed optima. Keep score updates finite and expose the policy in run settings; compare against the repaired current-learning control before making it the default.
5. **Make vitality rules deliberate.** Use wider arithmetic and resolve costs against actual available reserves. Recommended candidate: health saturates at a configured maximum instead of healing an organism to death; carried food cannot wrap or exceed its declared capacity. Preserve old vitality rules as a named comparison preset. Do not quietly reduce attack damage, increase all food or guarantee survival. Age-reduction actions, biological age, chronological lifetime and fertility are separate semantics: add immutable lifetime-in-seasons and generation for display now; defer a change to age/fertility clocks until death/birth measurements justify it.

The eight-action repertoire remains. UI descriptions can say “Consume reserves” (Eat), “Gather local food” (GenerateFood), “Reproduce” (CombineDna) and “Mutate” (Evolve), while tooltips retain code names and explain exact targets/costs. Neighbor-directed gathering, rejuvenation and mutation bonuses remain explicit current behaviors unless separately changed. This avoids accidentally changing the model merely by renaming a button.

### Predator–prey behavior

The owner explicitly wants this concept. The smallest proposed extension lets inherited actions support resource gathering, predation and mixed strategies within the existing organism model. A fixed two-species system, mandatory diets, kin immunity or enforced species quotas have not been chosen. Describe roles from actual feeding/attack outcomes, with mixed and inactive behavior visible; possessing a Kill gene alone does not establish a functioning predator.

Make the predation energy path explicit: an attack has a declared cost and damage, and successful feeding transfers a bounded amount of actual victim reserves through one resolved outcome. Prevent duplicate rewards for competing attackers and account for any reserves left on the cell or lost to an explicitly declared cost. This replaces the unreachable current Kill bonus with proposed model semantics, not a neutral bookkeeping fix. Freeze cost, damage, feeding benefit and conflict rules before comparative runs, then compare them without hidden rescue or forced population ratios. Exact constants remain proposal choices. Removing targeting/resource defects comes before deciding whether attacks are intrinsically too lethal.

## Part 3 — A descriptive, responsive viewer

Use a resizable/maximizable WinForms layout: the board occupies most of the window, an inspector sits beside it, and compact histories sit below. Add fit-to-window, zoom and pan while retaining the 100×100 simulation dimensions.

| Area | Proposed presentation |
| --- | --- |
| Board layers | Organisms, food heatmap with a 0–10 legend, and a combined view; optional selected-organism trail and brief action/birth/death markers. A cell can show resources even when occupied. |
| Pattern list | Named action chips, count and population percentage, plus “groups action types only; not fitness.” Clicking highlights matching occupants. A detailed signature includes target directions; legacy numeric codes remain optional. |
| Organism inspector | Stable run-local ID, position, parents/generation, lifetime, explicitly labeled biological/sequence ages, health, carried reserves, local food, offspring, eight gene slots with target arrows, chosen action, outcome and learned-versus-exploratory choice. Follow the organism as it moves and show its last observed death outcome. |
| Explanation | A bounded last-16-action trace for the selected organism, such as “Gather → own cell: +2 reserves, −2 local food” or “Move ↖: blocked.” No whole-population event archive or ancestry object graph. Parent IDs are scalar references; selection uses completed snapshots. |
| Population | Separate gene abundance from actual performed actions; show births/deaths, population, food and action activity over recent seasons. Explain “season,” “generation,” and the two age values. |
| Playback | Run, pause, single season, cycle batch, target speed and actual speed; optional fastest mode. Display speed and simulation speed are visibly separate. |
| Starting settings | Optional “New simulation” settings with random defaults, population density, seed/random-seed choice, initial local food, regrowth and starting reserves. Offer clearly named comparison presets and restore-defaults; group learning/vitality options under advanced settings where supported. Apply settings to a new run and show the active configuration. |

One worker owns simulation mutation. Publish a small detached view of a completed season; the UI reads that view, never live mutable organisms. Keep only the latest unpublished frame, not a backlog of every step. Build colors/food data off the UI thread, paint at a bounded cadence, and update expensive lists less frequently. Do not use the reflection-heavy full verification snapshot as the live frame format. Stable observation IDs, selection and visual effects must consume no simulation randomness.

Provisional local targets: 30-Hz display scheduling, 95th-percentile UI paint work below 33 ms and input acknowledgement below 100 ms on defined empty/default/full fixtures. These are acceptance targets to test, not current capabilities. Pause/reset acknowledge immediately and settle at a safe season boundary; elapsed stop latency is reported. If computation cannot produce new seasons quickly enough, report actual speed instead of implying that repainting an old frame is progress.

Measure and remove avoidable color-list/string/effect allocations. Compare simple serial loops with one bounded parallel layer; phases currently serialized by a global lock are the first candidates. Preserve coherent conflict resolution and test ordering/fairness. A scheduler change alters contested outcomes and random assignment; it is a declared model/comparability change, not a supposedly neutral speed optimization. GPU work, new UI frameworks and a larger simulated grid are deferred.

## Part 4 — Investigate a better starting setup

First compare the current random start against the repaired action/resource model with unchanged ecology settings. Then evaluate a maximum of three additional named candidates: a constrained-random founder repertoire with usable resource/reproduction actions, a food-setting adjustment, and the capped-health vitality candidate. Change one parameter family at a time. No hidden reseeding, immortality or automatic rescue.

A constrained-random preset still randomizes placement, directions and remaining genes, but it is not an unconstrained random population. Label it accordingly and make it optional. Unrestricted random initialization remains the default, as the owner requested; do not promote the constrained preset automatically after favorable results. Validate setting ranges and show chosen values/seed so a run can be reproduced. Mid-run mutation of ecological settings is deferred; playback and observation controls remain usable during a run.

Judge candidates using living population, founder and descendant turnover, generations achieved, births/deaths, action diversity, movement and resources. A longer-lived population alone is not sufficient: a frozen population of nonacting survivors also misses the owner objective. The owner rejects outcomes described as more than 90% of the board dying or one organism taking over. The default begins around 10% occupied, so empty-cell percentage cannot measure that intent. Cumulative deaths across successful generations also cannot measure collapse.

For review, propose reporting living population relative to both its initial count and its observed peak, alongside occupancy and the trajectory through each fixed horizon. A candidate collapse flag is a loss greater than 90% relative to the initial living population; peak-relative drawdown is a separate diagnostic. This denominator and the required duration/seed success rate remain to be agreed before ecological pass/fail claims. Report largest ordered action/target-pattern share and active feeding/attack diversity to expose behavioral domination. Do not use the current summed DnaCode as exact identity. Shared ancestry alone is not monoculture, especially with two-parent recombination; founder ancestry, if inspected, is a separate diagnostic. The owner has not specified a numeric takeover threshold. Record diversity/collapse outcomes honestly without promising that every unrestricted random world persists indefinitely.

Do not claim genetic adaptation merely from changing population size or individual learning. Report inherited repertoire/target changes separately from learned preferences. Demonstrating improved adaptation to a particular environment would require a more specific prospective comparison; this proposal aims first at understandable, functioning evolution and better observation.
